## Problem Statement

The user asked, as a product researcher would: "what problem should we solve, then build?"
The research answer reframes the question. Class-mode is not short on features — it has attendance,
fees, tests, AI grading, AI tutor, Study Arena, live classes, messaging, SIS, and more. For a
pre-PMF ed-tech tool, the documented cause of death is *adding* product, not lacking it
(85% of ed-tech fails on adoption, per the win-plan's own cited research).

The real problem sits underneath the demand: **the wedge has real traction the founder can neither
fully prove nor protect.** Teachers mark attendance daily, unprompted — genuine behavioral demand — but:
- **Prove (partial gap, narrower than it first looked).** The data of record already exists: the
  `attendance` table carries `marked_by` + `date` (one upsert row per student per day), a server-side
  `feature: "attendance"` event fires on every successful save (`attendance.ts:177`), and
  `scripts/metrics-weekly.ts:107` already reports `active_teachers` (`COUNT(DISTINCT marked_by)`) and
  `class_days_marked` from that table. What is **missing** is the granularity the Sep 30 gate needs:
  the metrics are school-level aggregates, not a **per-teacher × per-weekday matrix** ("teacher X marked
  Mon/Tue/Thu/Fri"), and there is no **≥60%-of-teachers-marking-≥4-days/week threshold check**. So the
  founder can see "N active teachers this week" but not "which teachers cleared the bar," which is the
  exact claim the gate requires. This is a reporting-query gap, not a missing-telemetry gap — and it is
  much cheaper to close than a new instrumentation pipeline.
- **Protect (real gap).** There is **no offline support**. `queryClient.ts:100` sets `retry: false`;
  there is no write queue, no local persistence, no cached app shell. On a cheap Android with patchy
  connectivity — the exact target environment — a teacher can mark 50 students, hit save, lose signal,
  and the day is silently lost behind an error toast. The habit erodes and the record is corrupted at
  the same time.

So the founder's belief ("teachers marking daily") and the database reality can quietly diverge on bad
networks — and both the Aug 14 payment and the Sep 30 gate are proven from the data, not from memory.

## Demand Evidence

**Strong, and newly confirmed this session.** The founder reports teachers marking attendance daily,
unprompted, at the pilot school (chosen over "marking only when I push," "barely used," and "I don't
know yet"). This is behavior, not interest — the demand evidence the 2026-07-15 doc explicitly said
was missing ("did not name any instance of dependence behavior").

**Honest caveat, surfaced under push:** the evidence is a "mix of both" — some numbers, some in-room
observation, but **not a clean per-teacher weekly picture the founder would stake the gates on.** This
gap is the direct motivation for the build. Aug 14 (first invoiced-and-paid) is 2 days out.

## Status Quo

At the pilot school, teachers mark attendance in Class-mode daily. The register is at or near primary-
record status for attendance. What the product does NOT yet do that the strategy requires:
- **Prove it (partly there):** `npm run metrics:weekly` already reports active-teacher counts and
  class-days-marked from the `attendance` table. What's missing is the per-teacher × per-weekday
  breakdown and the ≥4-days/week threshold — so the founder can't yet say "these specific teachers
  each cleared 4 days this week," which is the Sep 30 claim.
- **Protect it (not there):** no offline queue and no cached app shell; a dropped connection loses the
  day's marks with no retry/recovery, and the app can't even reload without a network.
- **Deliver the principal ritual:** principal must open the app and refresh manually; the founder is
  currently the delivery mechanism for the daily summary. (Out of scope here — next cycle.)

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

**Target user:** the teacher at the pilot school who marks attendance daily (the daily user whose habit
is the whole asset), and the founder (who must be able to *see* that habit to sell it and defend it).

**Narrowest wedge:** the single attendance-marking write path — the tap-to-save loop — made
**measurable** and **durable**. Not a new feature. Not the buyer loop yet. The one existing action the
company already depends on, made trustworthy end to end.

## Constraints

- Solo founder + AI agents; no venture capital; no new infrastructure or hires for this work.
- Target hardware: cheap Android phones, small screens (5"), patchy/intermittent connectivity.
- Vernacular (Hindi/Devanagari) is a real need but ranks below data integrity (premise 4).
- Aug 14 is a **sales action, not a build** — nothing shippable in 2 days changes whether the school pays.
- Attendance is a statutory-adjacent record: no silent data loss, no fabricated "saved" states, honesty
  invariant holds (never claim a mark landed when it didn't).
- Instrumentation event payloads carry role + workflow + timestamp only — no student PII.

3. **The highest-value build is integrity of the mark: prove it (per-teacher, per-day instrumentation)

4. **Vernacular and 44px touch targets rank below integrity.** Important, not urgent.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Prove-only (minimal viable)
Extend `metrics:weekly` to derive the per-teacher × per-weekday matrix and the ≥60%/≥4-days threshold
**directly from the `attendance` table** (`GROUP BY marked_by, date`), which is the source of record —
no new event, no schema migration, immune to client-side logging gaps. (The already-existing
`feature: "attendance"` save event at `attendance.ts:177` stays as-is; it is not the basis for the
adoption matrix.) Effort S (human ~half day / CC ~1 hr — it's a reporting query, not a pipeline). Risk
Low. Reuses `metrics-weekly.ts` and the `attendance` table. Pros: unblocks Sep 30 measurement now at
the exact granularity the gate needs; cheapest possible slice. Cons: does not stop data loss; and it
cannot measure the *offline*-loss rate — a save that fails offline never reaches the server, so the loss
is only quantifiable once the queue exists (Approach B).

### Approach B: Prove + Protect (ideal architecture) — CHOSEN (revised by eng review 2026-08-12 — see "Engineering Review" section below; the protect phase was de-scoped from a full service-worker app shell to local-persist + instrument-first, and the prove source moved from the mutable attendance table to the append-only feature_usage log)
Approach A, then the offline write path: optimistic UI, marks persisted locally (IndexedDB, localStorage
fallback), a cached app shell (service worker) so the app loads with no network, and background sync on
reconnect. Reconciliation is an **idempotent upsert keyed on the existing `UNIQUE (student_id, date)`
constraint** — replaying a queued mark twice produces the same row, which is how "exactly once" is
actually earned. The queue unit is a mark `(student_id, date, status, marked_by)`; because the DB row is
per-student-per-day (not per-class), a two-device same-day edit resolves as last-write-wins on the upsert
by default, with a review surface as a later refinement (this corrects the prior doc's per-class
"per-row partial-success" framing, which the schema does not support). This is the prior doc's E9 spec,
re-grounded in the real schema. Sequenced: reporting query first (Approach A), offline path second.
Effort L (human ~1.5-2 weeks / CC ~1-1.5 days, low confidence — correct offline sync, idempotency, a
service-worker shell, and hostile multi-device tests are genuinely where bugs live). Risk Medium.
Reuses Approach A + the attendance write path + the E9 spec.

### Approach C: Integrity + one buyer hook (creative/lateral)
Prove (A) + *light* protect (localStorage draft that survives reload/signal-drop, not full background
sync) + pull the **principal daily-summary one-pager** forward (auto-generate a printable/WhatsApp-able
absence digest so the principal gets a ritual and the founder stops being the delivery mechanism).
Effort M (human ~1 week / CC ~3-4 hrs). Risk Medium. Pros: touches the moat sooner. Cons: the offline
fix is half a fix (survives reload, not a truly offline day); splits focus across two loop stages.

## Recommended Approach

**Approach B, sequenced.** Approach A is literally B's first slice, so the Sep 30 proof lands in week 1
regardless. B maps directly to the session's stated goal — protect and prove the one habit the whole
company rests on — and kills the #1 friction the code audit found (silent data loss). The buyer loop
(principal ritual, term report, fee ROI) becomes the next office-hours cycle once the mark is trustworthy.

**Build sequence:**

1. **Week 1 — Prove (the slice that ships first, independently valuable):**
   - Extend `scripts/metrics-weekly.ts` with a query over the **`attendance` table**
     (`GROUP BY marked_by, date`, restricted to `role='teacher'`, reusing the existing
     `REAL_SCHOOL_SQL` / `notAdminSql` guards) that emits a per-teacher × per-weekday matrix
     ("teacher X marked Mon/Tue/Thu/Fri") plus a per-teacher weekly-days count and the
     **≥60%-of-teachers-with-≥4-marking-days** threshold result. No new event, no schema change.
   - Definitions fixed before running: "active teacher" = marked ≥1 class that school day; "marking day"
     = ≥1 attendance row authored by that teacher on that date; baseline week captured first; the ≥60% /
     ≥4-day thresholds written down as the pass/fail line.
   - Note the measurement limit honestly: this counts marks that **reached the DB**. Marks lost offline
     are invisible until the queue exists (step 2), so a low count could be low usage OR silent loss —
     which is itself the argument for step 2.

2. **Weeks 2-3 — Protect:**
   - Optimistic UI on the marking screen: the mark is committed to local state and shown as saved-local
     immediately, then reconciled with the server.
   - Persist unsynced marks to IndexedDB (localStorage fallback) so they survive reload and a fully
     offline stretch. **Cache the app shell via a service worker** so the marking screen loads with no
     network (required for the "reload while offline" success criterion — offline data without an offline
     app shell would still white-screen on reload).
   - Background sync on reconnect via **idempotent upsert on `UNIQUE (student_id, date)`**: replaying a
     queued mark is safe (same row), which backs the "exactly once" guarantee. Two-device same-day edits
     resolve last-write-wins by default; a conflict-review surface is a documented later refinement, not
     a week-3 requirement.
   - Honest state indicators: "saved on device / syncing / synced" — never a fake "saved" when it's only
     local. Honesty invariant holds.
   - Hostile tests: mark offline → kill network → reload (shell must load) → reconnect → assert every
     mark lands exactly once (idempotent replay); two devices editing the same class-day converge to a
     single deterministic row.

## Open Questions

1. **What does the week-1 per-teacher matrix actually show?** Whether the pilot already clears the
   ≥60%/≥4-day bar is unknown until the query runs. Either way it calibrates urgency — a clear pass means
   protect-work defends a proven habit; a near-miss means the offline gap may already be costing marks.
2. **IndexedDB vs. localStorage** as the primary offline store — decide at eng-review based on payload
   size and browser support on the target Android/WebView versions.
3. **Two-device conflict semantics** — default is last-write-wins on the `(student_id, date)` upsert; a
   review surface is a later refinement. Confirm this default is acceptable at eng-review (the schema's
   single-row-per-student-per-day design rules out true per-class-row merging).
4. **Offline app shell** — a service worker is in-scope for the protect phase (see build sequence). Lock
   the PWA/service-worker approach and its cache-invalidation story at eng-review.
5. Which pilot-school language (TD1) — still unblocks the vernacular pass, but that is out of scope here.

## Success Criteria

- **Prove (by end of week 1):** `npm run metrics:weekly` prints a per-teacher × per-weekday marking
  matrix (derived from the `attendance` table) plus the ≥60%/≥4-days threshold result against a baseline
  week. The founder can name which teachers cleared the bar from the data, not memory.
- **Protect (by end of week 3):** a teacher can mark a full class fully offline, reload the app (shell
  loads with no network), then reconnect, and every mark lands exactly once with no manual re-entry.
  Hostile tests pass. No screen ever shows "saved" for a mark that is only local-unsynced.
- **Gate impact:** by Sep 30, the founder can *prove* ≥60% of teachers marking ≥4 days/week at the
  per-teacher granularity the gate names (closing the aggregate-vs-per-teacher reporting gap), and the
  habit is no longer silently eroded by network failures.
- **No scope creep:** the buyer loop (principal summary, term report, fee ROI) and vernacular are
  explicitly NOT built in this cycle.

## Engineering Review (2026-08-12)

Six review-section issues raised and resolved, plus two cross-model tensions and six verified Codex
bug-findings folded in. Net effect: the **protect phase is de-scoped** (local-persist + instrument-first,
service-worker app shell deferred until failure data justifies it) and the **prove source moved to the
append-only `feature_usage` log**.

### Revised build sequence (supersedes the Recommended Approach sequence above)

1. **Week 1 — Prove:** derive the per-teacher × per-weekday matrix + ≥60%/≥4-days threshold from the
   **append-only `feature_usage` log** (dedup by `(user_id, day)`), NOT the mutable attendance table
   (Tension 2). Pilot-scope the query first (T1). `feature_usage.created_at` = real activity time, immune
   to attendance overwrite (Codex #1) and backfill inflation (Codex #4).
2. **Weeks 2-3 — Protect (minimal-first, Tension 1):** persist the class batch to IndexedDB (via `idb`)
   *before* POST; explicit "saved on device / syncing / synced" + retry UI (honesty invariant); drain
   batched **one POST per class-day** on reconnect (Issue 6); instrument the save-failure rate. Queue owns
   unsynced marks, server owns synced, React derives (Issue 5). Client sends `markedAt`; server clamps
   skew (Issue 2).
3. **Deferred until failure data justifies:** the service-worker app shell + offline reload (finding #9:
   needs cached auth + roster/data, not just a shell; #14: ~21MB precache, real-Android testing). Earn it
   from the measured save-failure rate.

### What already exists (reuse, do not rebuild)
- **Server offline primitive:** idempotent `ON CONFLICT (student_id,date) WHERE updated_at <= EXCLUDED`
  upsert (`pg-queries.ts:2712`) + client `markedAt` accepted (`attendance.ts:31`). Reuse.
- **Append-only usage log:** `feature_usage` fires on every save (`attendance.ts:177`). Becomes the prove source.
- **Aggregate metrics:** `metrics-weekly.ts:107`. Extend, don't rebuild.
- **IndexedDB precedent:** `ai-classroom.tsx:1160`. Reuse the pattern for the queue.
- **Batch write endpoint:** POST takes an array of marks per class-day. Reuse for batched drain.
- **Tests:** `attendance.test.ts` + two regression tests to extend.

### NOT in scope (deferred, with rationale)
- Service-worker app shell + offline app-reload — deferred until save-failure data proves it's needed (Tension 1).
- Offline authentication / cached `/api/auth/me` (finding #9) — rides with the deferred shell.
- Buyer loop (principal summary, term report, fee-recovery ROI) — next office-hours cycle.
- Vernacular / 44px touch targets — premise 4, ranks below integrity.
- New append-only save-audit table (Tension 2 option B) — `feature_usage` covers the need for now.
- Two-device conflict *review surface* — last-write-wins by clamped `markedAt` is enough at pilot scale.

### Failure modes (new codepaths)
- **Offline queue never drains** (device never reconnects within backfill window): T9 gives a terminal-failure state + teacher-visible recovery. Without it: silent permanent loss.
- **Replay after lost-ack**: T4 idempotency key prevents duplicate parent WhatsApp. Without it: parents spammed, honesty invariant broken.
- **Stale-replay false success**: T5 returns real count. Without it: "50 marked" shown when 0 applied — **critical silent gap** if T5 skipped.
- **Read failure seeds empty register**: T3 blocks marking. Without it: teacher overwrites a recorded day — **critical silent gap** if T3 skipped.
- **Shared-device bleed**: T8 namespaces + purges. Without it: one teacher sees/overwrites another's queued marks.

### Parallelization
- **Lane A (server/metrics):** T1 → T2 (shared `metrics-weekly.ts`, sequential). T3, T4, T5 touch `server/` — sequential within the lane to avoid `attendance.ts`/`pg-queries.ts` conflicts.
- **Lane B (client queue):** T6 → T7 → T8 → T9 (all share `attendance.tsx` / `attendance-queue.ts`, sequential).
- **Execution:** Lane A and Lane B are independent (server vs client) → run in parallel worktrees. T6 depends on T3/T5 landing (client relies on honest server responses) → start Lane B's T6 after Lane A's T3+T5 merge.
