## Problem Statement

The founder wants to build "an Anthropic for education": a durable, research-minded AI-education organization. The driving fear, stated across the session in four variants, is losing by being structurally weak — "the Lovable or Replit phase where I can lose," losing "because I was dependent" on frontier labs, or vanishing "in a night because someone has built it."

The session converged on one finding: every version of that fear has the same single defense — schools that depend on Class-mode, with per-student data that accumulates. The organization to build is therefore not a model lab or a standalone AI tutor (that layer is already free from OpenAI Study Mode, Google LearnLM/Guided Learning, and Claude for Education). It is the existing company, Class-mode, sharpened around an explicit thesis: **be the system of record and distribution channel through which frontier AI reaches real schools, with an Anthropic-style posture of honesty and rigor.**

## Demand Evidence

Thin, and acknowledged as the binding constraint. One pilot school live on production (Azure, classmode.inmodel.in). Real usage exists, but the founder did not name any instance of dependence behavior (a teacher chasing a fix, a term of real marks entered unprompted, paper registers retired). Two production AI-generation outages produced no recorded user complaints. No paying customer. Session assumption, uncorrected by the founder: "some real usage, no strong dependence evidence yet."

This is not a kill signal at pilot stage, but it means the org's first job is manufacturing dependence evidence, not scaling vision.

## Status Quo

Partially unknown — the founder did not enumerate which school workflows still run on paper/WhatsApp versus inside Class-mode (open question below). Known context: Indian schools in this segment run attendance, fees, marks, and parent communication on paper registers, WhatsApp groups, and memory. Incumbent school-ERP vendors (Teachmint, Classplus, LEAD, regional ERPs) exist, so the category is validated; the contest is segment-level execution and trust, not novelty.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

**Target user:** the principal (economic buyer) and teachers (daily users) of the current pilot school; by extension, principals of similar Indian schools reachable in person.

**Narrowest wedge:** one workflow — whichever of the four audited workflows (attendance, fees, marks, parent updates; Open Question 1) is closest to daily use — made the school's primary record, plus one paying principal. Tie-break: attendance (highest natural frequency). If the audit finds *no* workflow with real traction, that finding is itself the demand answer and goes straight into the willingness-to-pay conversation rather than being papered over. Not the AI tutor, not the platform. The AI features ride on the wedge; they are not the wedge. (Consistent with the existing project decision to gate the WhatsApp absence-alert pipeline on principal willingness-to-pay.)

"Primary record" is defined precisely because Indian state boards commonly mandate physical attendance registers and fee receipt books as statutory records: the criterion is that Class-mode is the first point of entry, and any mandated paper record is transcribed *from* Class-mode, never the reverse.

## Constraints

- Solo founder + AI agents; no venture capital assumed. Cannot and should not raise for frontier-model training.
- Price point must fit Indian school economics (order of ₹10–50/student/month, not $/seat SaaS).
- Cheap Android devices, patchy connectivity, vernacular languages.
- Free competition at the tutor layer (OpenAI Study Mode, Google Guided Learning) is permanent; plan assumes it.
- Current AI provider keys are fragile (free-tier Gemini, placeholder OpenAI key) — provider-agnostic design is already load-bearing.

## Premises

Agreed in session (choice "A" ratified the set):

1. **The moat is daily habit, earned trust, and trustworthy custodianship — not model quality, and not data-lock.** (Restated at the /autoplan gate, both models concurring, user approved.) The durable asset is distribution into schools plus the accumulating per-student record, whose value flows to the school and the future Lab; the data is exportable on demand because custody-worthiness IS the trust. Dependence evidence therefore means habit signals (daily unprompted use, pain when unavailable), never hostage signals. Never compete on "our AI is smarter."
2. **Models are swappable suppliers.** Provider-agnostic interface (Gemini-first with OpenAI fallback today; open-weight models as economics allow), own prompts, own evals, own data. No single-lab dependence.
3. **Demand evidence is currently thin.** One pilot school, no proof of dependence, no paying principal. The org is shaped to fix that first; research ambitions second.
4. **Anthropic's posture, not Anthropic's capital structure.** Honesty, safety, and rigor as culture and brand — e.g., the existing codebase invariant that fabricated dashboard data is never shown to real accounts — without frontier-lab capital requirements.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: The Sharpened Company (CHOSEN)
Class-mode is the organization; write its constitution and aim it. Deliverables: a one-page constitution (mission, the four premises as operating rules, an explicit "what we never build" list, and the Lab graduation trigger) plus a 90-day plan targeting the first paying principal and one workflow fully off paper. Effort S. Risk Low. Reuses everything that exists.

### Approach B: The Two-Layer Organization
One company, two named functions from day one: **Schools** (distribution, system of record, revenue) and **Lab** (learning maps, evals for real learning, honest publishing). The true "Anthropic for education" end-state shape. Rejected for now: with zero paying schools and low data volume, the Lab layer is organizational theater. Effort M. Risk Medium.

### Approach C: The Open Standard Play
Organize around an open per-student learning-record standard plus open evals for Indian K-12, Class-mode as reference implementation; become the neutral layer labs plug into (the MCP playbook). Highest ceiling. Rejected for now: standards need adoption to anchor them; one school is not adoption. Effort L. Risk High.

## Recommended Approach

**Approach A, with Approach B's constitution written into it.** A is B with honest sequencing. The constitution codifies the four premises so every future decision (feature, hire, partnership) can be checked against them, and it names the **graduation trigger**, defined once and used identically everywhere in this doc: **money received, or a signed written commitment with a named authorized signer, an amount, and a stated start date.** When the trigger fires, the Lab function is formally created (initially as a time allocation, not a hire), starting with the per-student learning-map schema and a "did the student actually learn" eval harness.

The constitution carries an **amendment clause** (added per cross-model review, so it guides rather than freezes pre-PMF choices): any never-build rule can be changed with a written rationale and a one-week cooling period between writing the rationale and acting on it. Deliberate reversals are allowed; impulsive ones are not.

The constitution's **"what we never build" list**, seeded from this session's own rejections:
1. No foundation-model training or frontier-lab ambitions (capital structure we cannot and should not raise for).
2. No standalone AI tutor app (that layer is free from OpenAI/Google; competing there is the Lovable trap).
3. No feature whose pitch is "our AI is smarter than X" (premise 1 — model quality is never the moat).
4. No single-provider dependence in any AI feature (premise 2).
5. No fabricated or demo data shown to real accounts (existing codebase invariant, now constitutional).

Anti-dependency design rule, enforceable in code review from day one: **the model is never the product; it is a swappable supplier behind an interface we own.** Multi-provider gateway (exists — `server/lib/ai/gateway.ts` is the single chokepoint), owned prompt templates (exist), owned evals (nascent — a pedagogy eval harness exists at `server/lib/eval/pedagogy.ts`; expand under the Lab trigger), owned longitudinal student record (to build — this is the compounding asset).

90-day objectives (in priority order):
1. **Willingness-to-pay conversation, run as a close attempt, in the right order** (revised per cross-model review): the school visit starts with the one-hour **workflow audit** (Open Question 1) so the ask is about the workflow the school actually lives in; then, same week, the conversation — ask the ₹20/student/month question, and in the same meeting present a **one-page paid-pilot offer** (amount, term, what's automated vs. founder-manual, and a signature line) and attempt the close, deposit or first invoice on the table. Also map the real buying process: ask directly who signs — principal, owner, trust, accountant. Price-anchor questions asked during the audit, before the number is spoken: "what does the school pay today for anything like this — ERP, SMS packs, printed diaries?" and "what did you stop paying for, and why?" The ₹20 anchor is calibrated against those answers, not asserted blind. A one-page buyer-alternatives memo (chains/trusts as single buyers, coaching centers, parent-pays) gets written from the audit data before day 30. **Parallel demand testing (approved at the /autoplan gate):** the same one-page offer runs at 3–5 additional schools in weeks 2–6, in parallel with pilot-school work — the playbook memo is not a post-mortem of school #1, it IS the experiment. One friendly-pilot answer is nearly zero information; five conversations is a demand signal. A "yes" is only the graduation trigger when it has a named signer with authority, an amount, and a stated start date. Captured in contemporaneous written notes (not audio). First attempt this week; hard fail line at day 30. **Consequence of the fail line (escalation, not freeze):** if day 30 passes without a written answer, build work (objectives 2–4 and all expansions) pauses, and the only permitted work is escalation — first to the mapped real buyer, then to a second school. Silence at school #1 redirects the learning window; it does not burn it.
2. **One workflow (per the wedge decision rule) becomes the school's primary record** for **30 consecutive school days** (holidays and non-school days excluded and non-resetting — day 90 falls in the October festival window, so calendar days would be mechanically impossible), unprompted. **"Primary record" has a denominator:** all sections/classes in scope, every school day, entries complete for the day, reconciled weekly against any statutory paper register (paper transcribed from Class-mode, never the reverse). One enthusiastic teacher does not satisfy it. **No-traction branch:** if the audit finds no workflow with organic use (the plan's own demand-evidence section predicts this is plausible — two outages, zero complaints), the streak objective converts to a re-onboarding experiment with a 2-week traction bar, and expansions 2.2–2.4 stay frozen until that bar is met — same escalation logic as the day-30 line, triggered at day ~7 instead of aiming build work at vapor. "Unprompted" defined: after a dated cutoff, no founder-initiated contact about that workflow; responding to inbound support questions is allowed and does not reset the clock; founder-initiated reminders or check-ins do. **Internal checkpoint: the workflow must be the primary record by day ~40** (roughly two weeks after the wedge audit), or this criterion is formally at risk and gets flagged, not quietly slipped. The 30-school-day streak must have *started* by day 55; because of the October festival calendar, its completion date is documented and may legitimately fall past day 90 — what cannot slip is the start.
3. **Dependence probe** — sequenced honestly, with the action stated plainly: **no induced outages; ship the metrics by day 14 (hard precondition deadline), then collect them weekly until day 90 via a founder-facing script (`npm run metrics:weekly` — a printout for an audience of one, not a dashboard), and treat any natural incident during the window as a probe event.** Metrics are **derived from existing domain tables first** (attendance rows, fee entries, and marks already carry author + timestamp — per-workflow daily active teachers needs no new pipeline), plus a minimal `usage_events` insert only for read/view actions that leave no domain row. Definitions are fixed before collection starts: what counts as an "active teacher" per workflow, a baseline week recorded first, and decision thresholds written down (e.g., "wedge DAU below 50% of baseline for two consecutive weeks = adoption is failing, act"). Event payloads carry role + workflow + timestamp only — no student PII. A logging failure must never break the serving request; the failure itself is logged. Gemini billing gets linked (small spend, un-confounds any AI-feature probe) but is explicitly **lower priority than all wedge work** — the wedge is administrative and does not depend on it. Prior data acknowledged: two natural AI-generation outages produced zero complaints — both negative. A third passive negative on AI features means "nobody depends on the AI features yet," full stop, and redirects all AI investment behind the wedge.
4. **Per-student learning-record schema draft is explicitly gated on the graduation trigger** (money received or signed written commitment — same trigger as the Lab, stated once in Recommended Approach). Not scheduled before it. Rationale, from this session's own observation: strategy work is this founder's documented escape hatch when demand questions get uncomfortable. If the principal says no, the schema draft does not happen; the refusal reasons get worked instead.

## Open Questions

These carry the two unanswered diagnostic questions from the session, written out in full:

1. **Status quo (session Q2, unanswered):** "At your pilot school today, what still happens on paper or WhatsApp instead of in Class-mode? Attendance, fee collection, marks entry, parent updates — which of these has actually moved into the product for real, daily, without you prompting them, and which are they still doing the old way?" First thing to establish on the ground; its answer selects the wedge workflow.
2. **Desperate specificity (session Q3, unasked):** "Name the actual human at the school who needs this most besides the principal — their name, their role, what gets them in trouble when the old way fails." The named champion.
3. What price does the pilot principal consider "obviously worth it," and for which workflow?
4. Whether the founder means to eventually pursue Approach C's open-standard ceiling once multiple schools are live.

## Success Criteria

- WTP close attempt: workflow audit first, then the conversation with the one-page paid-pilot offer, first attempt this week (slip condition: rescheduled within the week, not dropped); hard fail line day 30 for a written answer, with the escalation consequence defined in objective 1. Any substantive answer counts as success; silence or founder avoidance does not.
- By day 90: one paying school, OR a signed written commitment with a named authorized signer, amount, and stated start date (Indian school budget cycles anchor to the April session start — a July "yes, from next session" meeting that bar counts and fires the graduation trigger; a verbal maybe does not), OR a documented pivot decision. **Falsifiability rule:** a pivot decision only counts if it cites the refusal reasons verbatim from the written notes — "we pivoted" without the principal's words is a failed criterion, not a met one.
- Weekly time budget honored as a default: ~50% school-facing (audit, WTP, adoption support, school #2), ~30% build (metrics, term report, wedge-serving work), ~20% ops. Deviations are fine; invisible drift into build-only weeks is the failure mode being guarded.
- The wedge workflow is the school's primary record (per the transcribed-from definition) for 30 consecutive school days under the "unprompted" rules of objective 2: streak *started* by day 55 (checkpoints at day ~40 for primary-record status), completion documented even if the festival calendar pushes it past day 90.
- Constitution exists as a one-page document in the repo, **written the evening after the WTP conversation happens, whatever the answer — zero strategy artifacts before a principal has spoken** (the review's own observation: strategy work is this founder's escape hatch, and the constitution is the first calendar item that can't refuse him). It must contain the **segment paragraph**: the specific band (school size, board, geography, language) where a present, accountable, vernacular operator structurally beats Teachmint-class field sales and Google's free state-level distribution — and the growth ceiling that segment implies. If that paragraph can't be written, premise 1 fails now, not later. Premises cited in at least one real product decision, recorded in a `DECISIONS.md` alongside it.
- Per-workflow usage logging shipped and collecting the objective-3 proxy metrics weekly.
- **Deliberately excluded from the 90 days:** proving the provider swap under real traffic. The gateway exists and the premise matters, but spending money (a funded OpenAI account — the key is currently a placeholder) and taking UX risk on a never-load-tested provider path mid-dependence-sprint serves the paranoia premise, not the wedge. It becomes the first post-graduation-trigger engineering task, run as a 48-hour low-volume canary with a defined rollback (any quality or latency regression reverts immediately).
